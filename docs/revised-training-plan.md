# Revised training discussion

Source: user-supplied discussion, adopted 7 October 2026. The text below is retained as the program reference.

Yes. With even mild sciatica, I would **change the earlier plan rather than simply adding more ab work**. For the next 6–8 weeks, the priorities should be: build strong glutes/hips and legs, improve trunk stability, strengthen the upper/mid-back, and temporarily reduce exercises that place a lot of load or shear through the lumbar spine.

NICE recommends remaining active and using exercise as part of managing low-back pain with or without sciatica rather than defaulting to rest. [Nice](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/NG59/chapter/recommendations)

## Revised weekly plan

| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| Mon | Upper A + core stability |
| Tue | Lower A + hips/glutes |
| Wed | Recovery + back/core routine |
| Thu | Upper B + core stability |
| Fri | Lower B + hips/glutes |
| Sat | Optional arms/shoulders + easy cardio |

For now, train the major exercises around **2–3 reps in reserve**. This isn't the time to grind maximal squats or deadlifts.

# Monday — Upper A + Core

### Strength

| Exercise | Sets × Reps |
|---|---:|
| Dumbbell Bench Press | 4 × 6–10 |
| **Chest-Supported Row** | 4 × 8–12 |
| Incline Machine/Dumbbell Press | 3 × 8–12 |
| Neutral-Grip Lat Pulldown | 3 × 8–12 |
| Cable Lateral Raise | 3 × 12–20 |
| Face Pull | 3 × 12–15 |
| Triceps Pressdown | 2 × 10–15 |
| Dumbbell Curl | 2 × 10–15 |

I've specifically kept the row **chest-supported** rather than bent-over rows. You get plenty of back development without asking your lower back to hold a loaded hinge for multiple sets.

### Core

Do this slowly.

**1. Bird dog — 3 × 6–8 each side**

Don't lift the arm/leg as high as possible. Brace your abdomen and stop your pelvis rotating.

**2. Side plank — 3 × 20–40 sec each side**

Initially bend the knees if a full side plank is difficult.

**3. Pallof press — 3 × 10–12 each side**

Excellent for training your trunk to resist rotation.

---

# Tuesday — Lower A: back-friendly leg strength

This is the biggest change from the old program.

### 1. Goblet squat or belt squat
**4 × 8–12**

A belt squat is particularly useful if your gym has one because you can heavily train your legs with much less spinal loading.

I would temporarily choose this over heavy barbell back squats.

### 2. Hip thrust
**4 × 8–12**

Strong glutes are a major objective.

At the top:

- ribs down
- abdomen braced
- squeeze glutes
- don't hyperextend your lower back

### 3. Bulgarian split squat

**3 × 8–10 each leg**

Start relatively light.

Hold dumbbells beside you rather than loading a bar across your spine.

### 4. Seated leg curl

**3 × 10–15**

### 5. Leg extension

**3 × 10–15**

### 6. Standing calf raise

**3 × 10–15**

### Hip stability

**Cable hip abduction — 3 × 12–15 each side**

This works the glute medius/minimus, which contributes to pelvic stability during walking, running and single-leg movements.

### Strength

| Exercise | Sets × Reps |
|---|---:|
| Seated Dumbbell Shoulder Press | 3 × 6–10 |
| Pull-up / Assisted Pull-up | 3 × 6–10 |
| Machine Chest Press | 3 × 8–12 |

Notice that I changed standing overhead pressing to **seated pressing** initially.

It isn't because standing pressing is inherently dangerous. It simply removes another variable while your back is irritated.

### Core

**Dead bug — 3 × 6–10 each side**

Think about keeping the ribs and pelvis stable rather than trying to extend the leg as far as possible.

Then:

**Suitcase carry — 3 × 30–45 sec each side**

Carry one heavy dumbbell in one hand.

Your objective is to remain completely upright.

This is an excellent functional core exercise because the trunk must resist sideways bending.

# Friday — Lower B

### 1. Hack squat

**3 × 8–12**

Control the descent.

Don't force your knees all the way toward your chest if deep hip flexion causes sciatic symptoms.

### 2. Step-ups

**3 × 8–12 each leg**

Start with a relatively low box.

This develops:

- glutes
- quads
- hip stability
- single-leg control

### 3. Dumbbell Romanian deadlift — OPTIONAL

**3 × 8–10**

This one needs an important qualification.

If hinging produces **any increase in radiating leg pain, tingling or numbness**, remove it.

Start light and learn the hip hinge first.

If there's any doubt, replace it with:

**lying/seated hamstring curl — 3 × 10–15.**

I would **not do heavy barbell RDLs yet.**

### 4. Hip thrust / glute bridge

**3 × 10–15**

### 5. Leg curl

**3 × 10–15**

### 6. Calf raise

**3 × 12–15**

### Hip circuit

**Cable hip abduction:** 2 × 15 each side  
**Cable hip extension:** 2 × 12 each side

# Your most important core exercises

For your particular goal, I would prioritize **stability rather than repeatedly bending the spine under load**.

Your core program should therefore revolve around:

**Bird dog** → anti-rotation/stability  
**Side plank** → lateral trunk stability  
**Dead bug** → anterior core control  
**Pallof press** → anti-rotation  
**Suitcase carry** → real-world trunk stability  
**Modified curl-up** → controlled trunk endurance

Rather than doing hundreds of:

- crunches
- weighted sit-ups
- Russian twists
- decline sit-ups

Those aren't necessary for building a strong supportive trunk.

---

# Glutes and hips become a major priority

Think of your back-support system as:

**rib cage**
↓  
**lumbar spine + abdominal muscles**
↓  
**pelvis**
↓  
**glutes/hips**
↓  
**legs**

Your back shouldn't have to compensate for weak hips.

Over the coming months I particularly want you getting stronger at:

**Hip thrusts**

**Split squats**

**Step-ups**

**Hip abduction**

**Squat variations**

**Carries**

Those will collectively build a very solid base.

# Exercises I would temporarily avoid

Until the sciatic irritation is well controlled, I wouldn't make these staples:

- ❌ heavy conventional deadlift
- ❌ heavy barbell RDL
- ❌ heavy good mornings
- ❌ maximal back squats
- ❌ heavy bent-over barbell rows
- ❌ Jefferson curls
- ❌ weighted spinal twisting
- ❌ repeated loaded lumbar flexion
- ❌ explosive kettlebell swings
- ❌ very deep leg pressing if it rounds your lower back

I'm **not saying these exercises cause sciatica or are inherently bad**.

I'm removing them temporarily because there are easier ways to build the same muscles while you're symptomatic.

Eventually, some can be reintroduced.

# Before every lower-body session

Do this **8–10 minute preparation**:

**5 min brisk walk**

then

**Bird dog:** 1 × 6 each side  
**Glute bridge:** 1 × 12  
**Bodyweight squat:** 1 × 10  
**Hip abduction:** 1 × 12 each  
**Dead bug:** 1 × 6 each  
**Light split squat:** 1 × 6 each

Then start lifting.

No need for aggressive stretching.

In particular, don't repeatedly try to **stretch the sciatic nerve** by forcing yourself into deep forward folds because you feel tightness behind the leg.

---

# Very important rule: watch the leg symptoms

Muscle fatigue is fine.

What I care much more about is what happens to the **radiating symptoms**.

If an exercise causes pain/tingling/numbness to start travelling farther from the back/buttock and farther down the thigh, calf or foot, **stop that exercise and change the movement or range of motion**.

Likewise stop if you notice increasing numbness or weakness.

The NHS advises exercising appropriately for sciatica but stopping an exercise that causes pain and seeking advice if symptoms persist. [nhs.uk](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/exercises-sciatica-problems/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

---

# Progression

For the next few weeks, progression should be conservative.

For example:

**Goblet squat — 3 × 8–12**

Week 1  
20 kg: 10 / 10 / 9

Week 2  
20 kg: 11 / 11 / 10

Week 3  
20 kg: 12 / 12 / 12

Then:

22 kg: 8 / 8 / 8

Increase the weight **only if both the workout and the following day are okay**.

You don't get bonus points for adding weight while your leg symptoms are worsening.

---

# Once symptoms improve

After roughly **4–8 weeks of symptom-free or clearly improving training**, you can gradually rebuild the traditional movements:

**Goblet squat → safety-bar/front squat → back squat**

and

**Hip hinge drill → light dumbbell RDL → barbell RDL**

You don't actually have to return to conventional deadlifts unless you want to. They're an excellent exercise but **not mandatory for muscle or back health**.

## One medical caveat

Because you're describing actual sciatic symptoms rather than simple post-workout lower-back soreness, a **physiotherapist assessment would be worthwhile**, particularly if this has been recurring or persistent. Different things can produce sciatica-like symptoms, so the ideal rehab exercises aren't identical for everyone. NICE specifically recommends tailoring exercise to the person's needs and capabilities. [Nice](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/NG59/chapter/recommendations)

And get urgent medical assessment if you develop **new bowel/bladder problems, numbness around the groin/saddle region, rapidly worsening leg weakness, or severe symptoms affecting both legs**.

For you, I would make **strong glutes + strong legs + strong anti-movement core + strong upper back** the foundation of the muscle-building program. You can still put on substantial muscle while doing this; we're simply choosing exercises that give you a better risk/reward balance while the sciatica settles.

## Gym Hawk tracking choices

- The active routine is the 6–8-week support block. It does not advance or revert automatically.
- Wednesday is a loggable circuit with two rounds by default and a three-round option. Each exercise has one logged set per round. Do each exercise once, then repeat the circuit. Cardio is guidance rather than a lifting set.
- Friday defaults to three sets of hamstring curls in the optional hinge slot. This is separate from the later three-set leg-curl slot. Light dumbbell RDLs can be selected explicitly instead; the choice is saved in each session.
- Goblet squat, incline dumbbell press, Friday hip thrust and seated calf raise are defaults. Record substitutions in notes and keep equipment consistent when comparing loads.
- Saturday retains preacher/hammer curls and pressdown/overhead extensions for five total sets each of biceps and triceps. Rear-delt fly uses 15–20 reps. Farmer’s carries are removed from new Saturday sessions.
- Optional ramp-up caps working sets at two and targets about 3 RIR for strength exercises. Controlled core work has no RIR target. Wednesday rounds do not change with ramp-up.
- Rest timers are app defaults because this discussion omits rest prescriptions. Existing timers stay for unchanged movements; new compounds use 120 seconds, accessories 90 seconds, and core/circuit work 60 seconds.
- Per-side values apply to each side; log a set only after both sides are complete. Load-volume tracking does not double per-hand loads or per-side reps, and excludes timed, assisted and bodyweight-only work.
- Reaching the rep target is a conditional milestone. It does not establish that symptoms permit an increase. Only consider the smallest available increase if the workout and following day are okay.
- Four main strength workouts form the weekly target. Recovery and optional Saturday sessions are excluded.
