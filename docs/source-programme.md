# 16-Week Muscle-Building Programme

Source: user-supplied programme, 26 October 2026. Formatting and copied interface fragments have been cleaned; app-specific interpretations are documented in `16-week-programme.md`.

Beginner · Back-conscious · Calisthenics + Gym · Athletic Hypertrophy

The objective is to build a muscular, athletic physique with broader shoulders, a strong back, developed chest and arms, powerful legs, and a stable core—while minimising unnecessary stress on the lumbar spine.

At 60 kg and 5'11", the priority should be gaining muscle, improving movement quality, and strengthening the hips and trunk, rather than trying to become leaner.

**Duration:** 16 weeks: 4 foundation + 12 progression.

**Strength days:** 4–5, plus active recovery.

**Session length:** 60–90 minutes including warm-up.

The programme uses progressive resistance training, supported exercises, controlled calisthenics, and core stability. The 2026 American College of Sports Medicine position stand supports progressive training at least twice weekly per muscle group and highlights weekly training volume as an important driver of hypertrophy.

Important: This plan assumes mild, stable, non-progressive back discomfort. If pain is persistent, unexplained, radiates down a leg, or comes with tingling or weakness, get assessed by a physiotherapist or GP before beginning loaded training. The programme is general guidance, not a substitute for medical advice.

## 1. Weekly training split

Although six gym visits are possible, six hard lifting sessions are unnecessary for a sedentary beginner. This schedule builds in frequent training without compromising recovery.

| Day       | Training focus                            | Priority                   |
| --------- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Monday    | Upper A — chest, back, shoulders          | Hypertrophy                |
| Tuesday   | Lower A — quads, glutes, hamstrings, core | Strength + stability       |
| Wednesday | Easy cardio + mobility                    | Active recovery            |
| Thursday  | Upper B — pull-ups, dips, shoulders       | Calisthenics + hypertrophy |
| Friday    | Lower B — unilateral legs, hips, core     | Stability + hypertrophy    |
| Saturday  | Upper C — calisthenics, shoulders, arms   | Optional from Week 5       |
| Sunday    | Rest                                      | Recovery                   |

Weeks 1–4: Perform Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday. Use Saturday for walking or mobility only.

Weeks 5–16: Introduce Saturday as an optional, lower-fatigue fifth resistance session if the back and joints are recovering well.

### How to read the programme

- Tempo `3-1-1`: Lower the weight for 3 seconds, pause for 1 second, then lift in 1 second.
- RIR (reps in reserve): Finish the set feeling you could perform another 2–3 clean repetitions, rather than pushing to failure.
- Working sets: The sets shown exclude lighter warm-up sets. They represent the full programme from Week 5 onward; Weeks 1–4 use reduced volume as explained later.

## 2. Exercise programme

### Monday — Upper A: Chest and back

Primary goal: Build upper-chest thickness and back width.

About 65–80 minutes

| Exercise                             | Sets × reps | Rest | Tempo   |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------- | ---- | ------- |
| Incline dumbbell press, neutral grip | 3 × 8–12    | 120s | 3-0-1   |
| Chest-supported machine row          | 3 × 8–12    | 120s | 2-1-1   |
| Neutral-grip lat pulldown            | 2 × 8–12    | 90s  | 2-1-1   |
| Incline push-ups                     | 2 × 8–15    | 75s  | 2-0-1   |
| Cable lateral raise                  | 2 × 12–20   | 60s  | 2-1-1   |
| Rope triceps pushdown                | 2 × 10–15   | 60s  | 2-0-1   |
| Pallof press                         | 2 × 10/side | 45s  | 2s hold |

Technique priorities

Keep the lower back supported or stable throughout. Use a gentle natural arch during presses, without excessive lumbar extension. With chest-supported rows, keep the chest against the pad instead of pulling with the torso. For push-ups, keep the ribs and pelvis aligned.

If incline dumbbell pressing causes back discomfort, substitute a back-supported chest press machine.

### Tuesday — Lower A: Legs, glutes and core

Primary goal: Develop leg mass and strong glutes without heavy spinal compression.

About 65–80 minutes

| Exercise                 | Sets × reps  | Rest | Tempo   |
| ------------------------ | ------------ | ---- | ------- |
| Belt squat or leg press  | 3 × 8–12     | 120s | 3-0-1   |
| Supported reverse lunges | 2 × 8–10/leg | 90s  | 2-0-1   |
| Seated hamstring curl    | 3 × 10–15    | 90s  | 2-1-1   |
| Hip thrust machine       | 3 × 8–12     | 90s  | 2-1-1   |
| Seated calf raise        | 3 × 10–15    | 60s  | 2-1-1   |
| Dead bug                 | 2 × 8/side   | 45s  | Slow    |
| Bird-dog                 | 2 × 6/side   | 45s  | 5s hold |

Important modifications:

- Belt squats are a good option because the resistance is applied around the hips rather than directly across the shoulders.
- On leg presses, stop the descent before the pelvis rolls backward or the lower back loses contact with the seat.
- During hip thrusts, finish by extending the hips, not by arching the lower spine. Start with a floor glute bridge if necessary.
- Hold a rail or rack while lunging to reduce balance demands.

### Wednesday — Active recovery and mobility

This is not another heavy workout.

20–30 minutes: Easy cycling or walking

Comfortable conversational pace; no intervals or sprints.

15–20 minutes: Mobility

Hips, thoracic spine and gentle hamstring mobility from the warm-up section.

Because of the sedentary baseline, add short walking breaks throughout the day. Gradually work towards a comfortable daily step count without making walking another exhausting workout.

### Thursday — Upper B: Calisthenics and shoulders

Primary goal: Develop relative strength, wider lats, deltoids and arms.

About 65–80 minutes

| Exercise                              | Sets × reps     | Rest | Tempo |
| ------------------------------------- | --------------- | ---- | ----- |
| Assisted pull-ups                     | 3 × 5–8         | 120s | 2-1-1 |
| Back-supported machine shoulder press | 3 × 8–12        | 120s | 2-0-1 |
| Supported seated cable row            | 2 × 10–12       | 90s  | 2-1-1 |
| Assisted dips or chest press machine  | 2 × 6–10        | 90s  | 2-0-1 |
| Reverse pec deck                      | 2 × 12–20       | 60s  | 2-1-1 |
| Cable biceps curl                     | 2 × 10–15       | 60s  | 2-0-1 |
| Side plank                            | 2 × 20–40s/side | 45s  | Hold  |

Start with generous assistance for pull-ups and dips.

For dips, avoid descending so deeply that the shoulders roll forward or the lumbar spine extends excessively. If dips cause shoulder or back discomfort, use the chest press machine.

On the shoulder press, maintain contact with the backrest and avoid leaning back to complete repetitions.

### Friday — Lower B: Single-leg strength, hips and core

Primary goal: Improve hip control, single-leg stability and lower-body development.

About 65–80 minutes

| Exercise                 | Sets × reps  | Rest | Tempo |
| ------------------------ | ------------ | ---- | ----- |
| Supported low step-ups   | 2 × 8–12/leg | 90s  | 2-0-1 |
| Leg extension machine    | 3 × 10–15    | 90s  | 2-1-1 |
| Seated or lying leg curl | 3 × 10–15    | 90s  | 2-1-1 |
| Floor glute bridge       | 3 × 10–15    | 75s  | 2-2-1 |
| Hip abduction machine    | 2 × 12–20    | 60s  | 2-1-1 |
| Seated calf raise        | 2 × 12–20    | 60s  | 2-1-1 |
| Forearm plank            | 2 × 20–40s   | 45s  | Hold  |

Use a low step for step-ups, particularly during the first four weeks. Increase its height only when hip and pelvic control remain consistent.

The hip abduction machine strengthens the gluteus medius and related hip muscles, helping develop pelvic control during walking, running and single-leg exercises.

### Saturday — Upper C: Calisthenics and accessories

Optional from Week 5

Primary goal: Practise bodyweight strength and add low-fatigue upper-body hypertrophy.

About 45–60 minutes

| Exercise                 | Sets × reps | Rest | Tempo |
| ------------------------ | ----------- | ---- | ----- |
| Push-up variation        | 2 × 8–15    | 75s  | 2-0-1 |
| Inverted bodyweight rows | 2 × 8–12    | 75s  | 2-1-1 |
| Cable chest fly          | 2 × 12–15   | 60s  | 2-0-1 |
| Cable lateral raises     | 2 × 12–20   | 60s  | 2-1-1 |
| Hammer curls             | 2 × 10–15   | 60s  | 2-0-1 |
| Rope triceps pushdowns   | 2 × 10–15   | 60s  | 2-0-1 |

Start with only one set per exercise in Week 5. Build towards two sets if recovery is good.

From Week 9, an optional calisthenics core skill can be added:

Supported captain's-chair knee raises

2 × 6–10 controlled reps, only if symptom-free and capable of avoiding swinging or excessive spinal movement. Otherwise, continue with dead bugs.

## 3. Core training: Build a strong, stable trunk

A strong core does not simply mean strong abdominal muscles. It means being able to control movement and manage forces through the trunk while the arms and legs work.

Anti-extension — Dead bug and plank

Resist excessive arching of the lower back. Exhale gently while extending the leg. Keep the trunk still.

Tuesday and Friday

Anti-rotation — Pallof press and bird-dog

Resist rotation while a cable or moving limb challenges trunk stability.

Monday and Tuesday

Anti-lateral flexion — Side plank

Resist bending sideways. Maintain a straight line through shoulders, hips and ankles.

Thursday

### Core progression

| Stage       | Progression                                                                          |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Weeks 1–4   | Dead bugs with bent knees, short side planks, basic bird-dogs                        |
| Weeks 5–8   | Longer holds and more controlled limb extension                                      |
| Weeks 9–12  | Stronger Pallof resistance, longer-lever planks and bird-dogs                        |
| Weeks 13–16 | More difficult lever positions or small resistance increases, without losing control |

Do not progress all core exercises simultaneously. Prioritise breathing, stable positioning and symptom-free repetitions rather than maximum hold times.

## 4. First four weeks — Movement foundation

The biggest mistake for a sedentary beginner is using too much volume before tissues and technique have adapted.

| Week | Focus | Working sets | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Learn | 2 sets main lifts; 1–2 accessories | 4 RIR |
| 2 | Repeat | 2 sets; improve control and reps | 3–4 RIR |
| 3 | Build | 3 sets main lifts; 2 accessories | 2–3 RIR |
| 4 | Consolidate | Maintain sets; refine technique | 2–3 RIR |

The sets shown above are working sets. Core exercises remain at approximately two short sets throughout this foundation phase.

The first month should prioritise:

1. Learning exercise setup, comfortable range of motion and breathing.
2. Discovering resistance levels that allow excellent technique.
3. Establishing consistency across four lifting days.
4. Monitoring whether back symptoms change after sessions.

Success at Week 4: You can complete four weekly sessions with controlled technique, predictable recovery and no sustained increase in back symptoms. Increasing weight is a secondary goal.

## 5. Twelve-week progressive roadmap

This begins after the four-week foundation. The complete programme therefore covers 16 weeks.

### Phase 1 — Weeks 5–8

Hypertrophy base

Use the full prescribed volume for four strength days. Introduce Saturday gradually. Accumulate repetitions, maintain 2–3 RIR and use small weight increases when appropriate.

Focus: consistency, technique and gradual muscle growth.

### Phase 2 — Weeks 9–12

Strength and calisthenics progression

Progress towards less-assisted pull-ups and dips, lower-incline push-ups and more difficult inverted rows. Continue increasing resistance on supported gym exercises.

Focus: improved relative strength and controlled progressive overload.

### Phase 3 — Weeks 13–15

Muscle-building consolidation

Continue small load or leverage increases. If recovery is consistently good, add one weekly set to a lagging muscle group, such as chest, lats or side delts—not every exercise.

Focus: sustainable increases in strength and training volume.

### Phase 4 — Weeks 16

Deload and reassessment

Reduce working sets by roughly 40–50%. Use comfortable loads, keep 4+ RIR and skip optional intense calisthenics.

Focus: recover, review progress and prepare for the next training block.

### Exactly how to progress your exercises

| Exercise type             | Progression method                                                                             |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Machines and dumbbells    | Build reps within the prescribed range, then increase load by the smallest available increment |
| Pull-ups                  | Gradually reduce assistance; progress towards unassisted repetitions                           |
| Dips                      | Reduce assistance before considering added weight                                              |
| Push-ups                  | Lower the hand support height; then progress from standard to feet-elevated push-ups           |
| Inverted rows             | Move feet further forward to make the body more horizontal                                     |
| Planks                    | Increase hold time, then lever length, rather than adding heavy weights                        |
| Dead bugs and bird-dogs   | Extend limbs farther while maintaining trunk control                                           |
| Split squats and step-ups | Increase stable, pain-free range and reps before load                                          |
| Glute bridges             | Add a band or modest machine resistance only after controlling pelvic position                 |

The preferred hypertrophy progression is double progression: increase repetitions first, then load.

For example, using incline dumbbell press at 3 × 8–12:

**Progression example:** Incline dumbbell press at 10 kg per hand, with 8 reps on each of three sets: keep the same weight and work towards 12 clean reps on all three sets before increasing resistance.

This is a progression illustration, not a recommended starting weight. Use the smallest increment available, often 1–2 kg per dumbbell.

If you cannot increase weight without pain or losing technique, progressing repetitions, control, or bodyweight leverage is still productive. There is no requirement to train to muscular failure.

## 6. Warm-up, mobility and cool-down

### Before every training session — 10–15 minutes

| Step | Exercise                               | Duration |
| ---- | -------------------------------------- | -------- |
| 1    | Easy treadmill walking or cycling      | 4–5 min  |
| 2    | Gentle hip flexor mobility             | 30s/side |
| 3    | 90/90 hip switches                     | 6/side   |
| 4    | Side-lying thoracic open books         | 6/side   |
| 5    | Gentle dynamic hamstring movements     | 8/side   |
| 6    | Glute bridges or dead bugs             | 1 × 8    |
| 7    | Light warm-up sets for first main lift | 2–3 sets |

For the first lifting exercise, begin with a very light resistance and gradually approach the working weight without fatigue.

### Mobility exercise references

Half-kneeling hip flexor stretch

Gentle pelvic tuck, ribs down; avoid arching the lumbar spine.

90/90 hip switches

Move between sides slowly; support yourself with your hands.

Thoracic open books

Rotate through the upper back while keeping the pelvis relatively stable.

Gentle hamstring mobility

Slightly bend the knee and avoid forceful stretching or neural tension.

If stretching the hamstrings causes tingling, burning, or pain running down the leg, stop that stretch. These symptoms may reflect nerve sensitivity rather than simple muscle tightness.

### Cool-down — 5–8 minutes

Finish with a few minutes of gentle walking, easy hip flexor or chest stretches as tolerated, and 1–2 minutes of slow relaxed breathing. The purpose is to transition out of training comfortably, not to force flexibility improvements.

## 7. Nutrition for lean muscle gain

Because the client starts at approximately BMI 18.5, gaining body mass is desirable. A lean, athletic physique still requires enough calories to build muscle.

**Calorie surplus:** +200–300 kcal/day above maintenance.

**Protein:** 100–130 g/day, approximately 1.6–2.2 g/kg.

**Weight gain target:** 0.15–0.3 kg/week on average.

**Sleep:** 7–9 hours each night.

A protein intake around 1.6 g/kg/day is supported by evidence for maximising resistance-training adaptations in many healthy adults, with 2.2 g/kg/day providing a practical higher-end target.

### Simple food choices

| Food                           | Why include it                          |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------- |
| Eggs, chicken, fish            | High-quality protein                    |
| Dal, chickpeas, tofu           | Protein, fibre and additional nutrients |
| Rice, oats, potatoes           | Carbohydrates for training energy       |
| Lactose-free milk or yoghurt   | Convenient protein and calcium          |
| Nuts, olive oil, peanut butter | Calorie-dense fats                      |
| Fruit and vegetables           | Fibre and micronutrients                |
| Whey isolate, if tolerated     | Convenient way to fill a protein gap    |

Spread protein across three or four meals where practical.

Track average morning body weight across several days rather than reacting to one measurement. If body weight does not rise for two or three weeks, increase daily intake by approximately 100–150 kcal. If weight increases faster than intended, reduce slightly.

Protein powder is optional; consistent food intake matters more.

## 8. Back-safety rules

### Prefer these exercises over heavy spinal loading

| Limit or avoid initially         | Back-conscious alternative                           |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Barbell back squat               | Belt squat, controlled leg press                     |
| Conventional deadlift            | Hamstring curl and hip thrust/glute bridge           |
| Bent-over barbell row            | Chest-supported row                                  |
| Good morning                     | Glute bridge and hamstring curl                      |
| Heavy unsupported overhead press | Back-supported machine shoulder press                |
| Weighted sit-ups                 | Dead bugs, planks                                    |
| Heavy side bends                 | Side planks and Pallof presses                       |
| Swinging hanging leg raises      | Dead bugs or supported bent-knee raises if tolerated |

These are substitutions for this individual's circumstances, not claims that the original exercises are inherently dangerous.

### Essential form rules

- Keep the spine in a comfortable, controlled position. Neutral does not mean rigidly immobilised.
- Brace gently through the abdominal wall, as though preparing for a light push against the stomach.
- Exhale during exertion rather than holding your breath through long, strained repetitions.
- Avoid using torso momentum to complete rows or presses.
- Do not force a deeper range of motion if it causes the pelvis to roll or the back to become uncomfortable.
- Select loads that allow consistent technique from the first repetition to the last.

### Pain decision guide

Continue cautiously

Ordinary muscle effort, mild fatigue or familiar non-worsening discomfort that settles promptly. Monitor how you feel later and the next morning.

Stop the exercise and modify

Sharp pain, increasing lumbar pain, new symptoms extending into the buttock or leg, or discomfort that is clearly worse after training. Reduce load or range of motion, substitute an exercise and get professional advice if symptoms persist.

Seek urgent medical assessment

New or worsening neurological symptoms need attention. Back pain with numbness around the genitals or anus, changes in bladder or bowel control, or pain/tingling/weakness in both legs warrants immediate emergency assessment in A&E or via 999.

The NHS advises remaining active with ordinary back pain but stopping exercises that worsen symptoms and seeking medical guidance when necessary.

An appointment with a musculoskeletal physiotherapist is particularly worthwhile before progressing to harder calisthenics or heavier resistance if there is a history of sciatica or recurrent back pain.

## 9. What to track during the programme

Progress should be measured through strength, body weight, physical changes, and symptom stability—not simply how tired a workout makes you.

### Weekly habits checklist

- Complete four main strength sessions.
- Reach the daily protein target.
- Maintain a calorie surplus.
- Take movement breaks during sedentary hours.
- Sleep 7–9 hours.
- Record weights, reps and back symptoms.

Track weekly bodyweight averages and repetitions and resistance for every working set. The supplied text also suggested photos every four weeks; photos and photo reminders are excluded from Gym Hawk by user choice.

## 10. Realistic outcomes after 16 weeks

If training, recovery and nutrition are consistent, reasonable aims include gaining approximately 2–4 kg of body weight, improving pushing and pulling strength substantially from the beginner baseline, increasing shoulder and back development, and gaining better control of the pelvis and trunk.

Those are targets rather than guarantees. Weight gain will include varying proportions of muscle, fat, glycogen and water, and back discomfort may require adjustments or professional treatment.

The most important principle: Build muscle through progressive overload, not progressively heavier spinal loading. For this client, clean repetitions, supported movements, steady calorie intake and pain-aware progression are a better foundation for an athletic calisthenics-style physique than rushing into advanced lifts.
